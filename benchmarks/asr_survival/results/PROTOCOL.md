# PROTOCOL - Survival of disfluency evidence in larger / verbatim ASR models (EXPLORATORY)

Written 2026-11-04, frozen (sha256 in `PROTOCOL.sha256.json`) and committed BEFORE the first decode of any system in section 3. Self-review by the executing agent; no independent review. DIHANA fold 5 is never read. No default of the engine or service changes. Requested by simulated reviewer R2 ("is the survival finding tied to Whisper small int8?"); this is an extra check, not a new claim.

## 0. Question

The paper reports survival (how much of the human-marked disfluency evidence is still present in the ASR transcript) for Whisper small CPU int8 with four decoding variants on a 1025-turn DIHANA frame. Does the finding "the ASR erases most literal repetitions, revisions and non-lexical filled pauses" hold with (a) a larger Whisper (large-v3) under the SAME prompts and (b) a model trained to transcribe verbatim (CrisperWhisper 2.0)? Same frame, same metrics, same bootstrap.

## 1. Known to the author before freezing (declared, not hidden)

- The small-model transcripts of P0, P1, C1 and p_none for the 1025 frame units exist in cache (`results/asr_recovery_20261031/asr/`, `results/control_noprompt_20261103/runs/asr/p_none/`) and are reused, never re-decoded. Their published values: literal-repetition survival 105 / 144 / 42 / 48 of 214; revision survival 53 / 58 / 19 / 24 of 161; non-lexical filler survival 158 / 108 / 6 / 7 of 308 (P0 / P1 / C1 / p_none).
- Feasibility smoke tests (no metric computed, nothing saved as a result): (i) faster-whisper large-v3 on GPU, variant p_none, on the first 20 frame units; the author read the text of the first 3 (short questions about train fares); (ii) CrisperWhisper 2.0 turbo and large on 23 DIHANA turns of folds 1-2 that are NOT in the frame, to see the output format. Seen: CrisperWhisper writes filled pauses as bracketed upper-case tags (`[UH]`, `[UM]`), keeps word fragments with a hyphen (`de- de`), writes Spanish accents and `¿?` correctly, and takes about 2-3 s per short turn on this GPU. No frame turn was decoded with CrisperWhisper.
- The analysis script was run once as a code check with the small-model directories standing in for the missing systems (`ASM_DRYRUN`, output in a scratch directory, never a result). It reproduced the published small-model values above.
- Expectation of the author (not a result, changes no rule): large-v3 is also trained on cleaned web transcripts and is expected to drop most non-lexical fillers without a prompt, so survival without a prompt should stay low; whether a larger model keeps more repetitions is uncertain. CrisperWhisper is expected to keep more filled pauses (as tags) and more repetitions.

## 2. Frame (frozen, DIHANA folds 1-4 only)

`results/asr_recovery_20261031/subset.json`: 1025 user turns, 122 speakers; 214 `rep_literal` positives (84 speakers), 161 `revision` positives, 679 sampled negatives; 308 turns with a non-lexical `<f>` tag (`f:a`, `f:e`, `f:m`, `f:sin_identificar`; column `raw_tag_counts` of `results/baselines_20261030/dihana/turns.csv`). ## 4. Metrics (code reused unchanged from `scripts/control_noprompt/analyze_control.py` and S2; adapter in `scripts/asr_survival_models/analyze_models.py`)

4.1 **survival_rep (primary)**: `turn_survives('repetition', rep_marks, text, {'A_literal_repeat'})` on the 214 positives. **survival_rev**: `turn_survives('revision', ...)` on 161. **survival_filler**: share of the 308 filler-positive turns whose scored text contains at least one non-lexical filled pause (`transcript_reference.is_nonlexical_filler`; `este` and conjunction `e` excluded); turn level.
4.2 Descriptive per system: pooled WER vs reference text A on 1025 units and on the 679 negatives (WER is not a recovery signal; text A has no non-lexical fillers, so an emitted filler is an insertion), insertions per reference token on negatives, S2 loop clips (`is_loop_clip`), `rule_rep` false positives on negatives, filled-pause emission on the 717 filler-absent turns, adjacent identical token pairs on negatives, empty outputs, mean words, decode seconds per audio second (the GPU and CPU RTFs are not comparable).
4.3 **CrisperWhisper tags (frozen before decoding)**. The output contains bracketed tags. (a) Primary scoring text: every `[...]` tag removed (tags are markup, not words of text A) for survival_rep, survival_rev, WER, loop, rule FP. (b) Filled-pause tags counted for survival_filler: tag content, lower-cased, in {uh, um, eh, em, er, ah, ahh, hm, hmm, mm, mmm}; a turn counts as filler-surviving if it has such a tag OR a non-lexical filler word by the standard rule in the stripped text. Other tags (e.g. `[laughter]`) are not fillers; their counts are reported. (c) Sensitivity `cw_large_tagtext`: tags kept as ordinary words (`[UH]` -> `uh`) in the text used for survival_rep / survival_rev / WER, to show whether the tags break adjacency of a repetition; filler counting unchanged. A tag is a different construct from Whisper emitting the word "eh": filler survival of CrisperWhisper is therefore reported but compared with Whisper only descriptively.

## 5. Statistics

Paired differences on identical units; ratio-of-sums bootstrap over speakers (`boot_ratio` of S2), 5000 resamples, seed 20261101, 95 % percentile CI, two-sided bootstrap p (floor 1/(B+1)); per-system intervals use the same bootstrap. **Holm family of 20** = 10 contrasts x {survival_rep, survival_filler}: lv3_P0 - small_P0; lv3_P1 - small_P1; lv3_C1 - small_C1; lv3_pnone - small_pnone; cw_large - small_P0; cw_large - small_pnone; lv3_P1 - lv3_P0; lv3_P0 - lv3_pnone; lv3_P1 - lv3_pnone; lv3_P1 - lv3_C1. Everything else (survival_rev, WER, loops, rule FP, tag sensitivity cw_large_tagtext - cw_large) is descriptive, without Holm. Because the floor of the bootstrap p is 2/5001, the smallest possible Holm-adjusted p in this family is 0.008.

## 6. Hypotheses and decision rules

6.1 **Reading of a survival interval** (applied to survival_rep, survival_rev, survival_filler of each system): CI upper < 0.50 = "majority erased"; CI upper < 0.75 = "erasure persists" (at least a quarter of the marks is lost, supported by the interval); CI lower >= 0.75 = "substantially attenuated"; otherwise "inconclusive". The 0.75 and 0.50 thresholds are conventions of this protocol, not derived from data.
6.2 **D2 (generalization of the repetition finding)**: the finding generalizes if survival_rep reads "erasure persists" or stronger for BOTH lv3_P0 (the production-prompt analogue) and cw_large. "Partly" if only one does; "no" if neither. The same reading is reported for revisions and filled pauses without a rule attached.
6.3 **D3 (P1 trade-off replicates on large-v3)**: replicates if lv3_P1 - lv3_P0 has survival_rep CI lower > 0 and Holm p < 0.05 AND survival_filler CI upper < 0 and Holm p < 0.05; "partly" if one of the two; "no" otherwise.
6.4 Model effects (lv3 vs small, cw vs small) carry no directional hypothesis: reported with CI and Holm p. A null or negative result is reported as such.

## 8. Limits declared in advance

One corpus (DIHANA, telephone, peninsular Spanish), one frame sampled for repetition / revision; survival is a transcript heuristic, not a human judgement (the kit in `validacion_manual/` is the way to measure its validity and has not been run); device and precision differ between small and the new systems; CrisperWhisper filler tags are a different construct from Whisper filler words; CrisperWhisper 2.0 is a recent model whose training data and Spanish coverage were not audited here; no model other than large-v3 and CrisperWhisper 2.0 large is tested; not a confirmatory result.
